## 1. GAME MOTHER CHAPTER (book 204–219)

### 1.1 Game principles and themes (GM guidance, non-mechanical) [p. 206–210]
- Eight principles (names only, no mechanics): 1 Riff from the movies, 2 Limit their resources, 3 Stay in the shadows, 4 Increase the pressure, 5 Let them breathe, 6 Fuel their agendas, 7 Bring horrible death, 8 Reveal the universe; plus sidebar "Knowing the unknowable" [p. 206–207].
- Three themes: Space Horror (sub-themes Space is Hell, Killing Greed), Sci-Fi Action (Someone Else's War, Expendable Assets), Sense of Wonder (The Origin of Life, Old and New Gods); plus Retro Futurism aesthetic [p. 208–210]. No mechanics. (Campaign Focus table in 1.6.4 reuses some names.)

### 1.2 Stress and horror (brief; panic is specified elsewhere) [p. 211–213]
- GM's primary tools for terror are Stress and Panic (rules p. 103). Stress is mainly incurred by the PCs through their own actions (pushing rolls), and secondarily introduced by the GM when PCs encounter horrific situations or beings [p. 211].
- Three horror tools: Fear of the unknown, Loss of control, Horrible choices [p. 211].
- Three stages of fear (pacing guidance): DREAD (use as often as possible) → TERROR (use selectively) → HORROR (the reveal; at that point play switches to the rules: draw initiative, then fight or flee; use rarely, best late in a session) [p. 212].
- Relieving stress: players can reduce Stress Level via signature item, safe place to hide/rest, helping each other calm down (rules p. 104). GM should allow calmer periods, then strike again [p. 213].
- Splitting the team: allowed; GM cuts back and forth between groups; all players stay at the table; consider what communication means PCs have (personal communicators / terminals) [p. 213].

### 1.3 Agendas, secrets & PvP [p. 213]
- Every PC's Personal Agenda is secret, known only to that player and the GM.
- A player may secretly communicate to the GM (recommendation: write a note, stay in the room). Some groups talk about secrets openly, separating player knowledge from PC knowledge.
- If a PC starts to act in direct opposition to the rest of the group, the GM "calls PvP": play the current scene to an end, then the PC becomes an NPC (under GM control). (Cinematic detail: p. 32, see 1.5.4.)

### 1.4 NON-PLAYER CHARACTERS [p. 214] (+ NPC rules collected from other chapters)

#### 1.4.1 Creating NPCs [p. 214]
- Typical NPCs are listed on p. 354–357 (section 2.5); pick one or use as a basis.
- ATTRIBUTES: typical NPCs total 14 attribute points (same as PCs); guideline, not a hard rule. **Max attribute rating 5 for any NPC; androids exempt.**
- SKILLS: only assign skill levels the NPC is likely to need. Fairly competent NPC = skill level 2; experts = 3, 4 or even 5.
- TALENTS: NPCs usually have none; an especially capable NPC may be given one.
- Four required descriptive fields for every NPC besides stats: NAME, APPEARANCE (looks, behavior, quirks), TRAIT (one defining characteristic, e.g. cynical, rational, ruthless), GOAL.
- Printed NPC stat format (p. 355–357, 367, 374): Name / role, STRENGTH, AGILITY, WITS, EMPATHY, HEALTH, SKILLS (only non-zero ones listed), TALENT(S), GEAR. Scenario NPCs additionally list AGE and CAREER. No printed sample NPC has an agenda, Stress Level or signature item.
- Observation (not a stated rule): in every printed NPC block, HEALTH = STRENGTH (same as PC rule, p. 30).

#### 1.4.2 Playing NPCs [p. 214]
- NPCs are governed by the same rules as PCs, but the GM may disregard those rules as long as the NPC is not directly interacting with a PC.
- **Do not track consumables or gear for NPCs** (GM decides when they run out).
- **Do not roll dice for NPC actions unless they attack or interact with a PC.**
- NPCs IN GROUPS: multiple NPCs can perform actions as a group instead of individually, using the helping rules (p. 63).

### 1.5 CINEMATIC PLAY [p. 215–216] (+ cross-references)

#### 1.5.1 Mode definition [p. 19, 215]
- Short, focused, single-session (usually) scenario emulating one ALIEN film; high stakes, PvP likely, most PCs not expected to survive [p. 19].
- Comes with pregenerated characters tailored to the scenario ("roles in a script") [p. 215]. Recommended before Campaign play; good for beginners and conventions [p. 215].
- Players choose which pregen to play; if they can't agree, roll dice; leftover characters become GM-run NPCs [p. 26].

#### 1.5.2 Structure of a Cinematic scenario [p. 216]
Components (data model for a scenario):
1. PROLOGUE — read-aloud intro for players.
2. THE SITUATION — GM-only overview of what's really happening.
3. THE CHARACTERS — stats + background for PCs and NPCs. **Every PC gets a separate Personal Agenda per Act (Acts 1–3).**
4. LOCATIONS — detailed map(s) with key locations.
5. ACTS & EVENTS — three Acts:
   - ACT 1: THE SETUP — PCs and NPCs introduced, conflicts established.
   - ACT 2: THE TILT — something unexpected raises stakes and danger; secrets revealed.
   - ACT 3: THE SHOWDOWN — climactic resolution depending on PC actions.
   - Each Act has a number of possible Events: some may be mandatory, most optional; generally stand-alone (not a predetermined chain). Some Events affect all PCs, others target specific PCs. **Some Events can change a PC's Personal Agenda.**
6. EPILOGUE — suggested sign-off message by one surviving PC (read aloud or adapted).

No trigger rule is given for moving from one Act to the next; Act transitions are driven by the scenario text / GM. **AMBIGUOUS:** app must let the GM advance Acts manually.

#### 1.5.3 What changes per Act
| Item | Rule | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Personal Agenda | Start of every Act: GM hands every PC a NEW Agenda for that Act (handout). Agendas are hidden: not shown to other players, NOT written on the character sheet. | p. 31, 216 |
| Agenda evaluation → Story Point | At the end of each Act, GM evaluates each PC. If the player advanced their Agenda in spite of personal risk or significant sacrifice (p. 31: "took any specific actions to further your Agenda during the Act"), GM announces it and awards **1 Story Point**. GM does not say why. Agendas remain hidden until the end of the scenario. | p. 31, 216 |
| Story Point cap | A player can never have more than 3 Story Points. | p. 61, 383 |
| Story Point ownership | Story Points belong to players, not PCs; players may keep them for the next Cinematic scenario (cap 3 still applies). | p. 383 |
| Story Point use | In a Cinematic scenario, spend 1 Story Point to get one automatic `[SUCCESS]` on a dice roll — either after a failed roll, or after a successful roll to get an extra `[SUCCESS]`. **DECIDED:** Cinematic play only; at most one Story Point per roll; skill rolls only. | p. 61 |
| Signature item | Once per Act (Cinematic) / once per game session (Campaign): interact with your signature item to reduce STRESS LEVEL by one step; typically a slow action. Some conditions (Starving, Freezing) can block stress relief. | p. 104 |
| Talent "The Long Haul" (Roughneck) | Once per Act (Cinematic) / once per session (Campaign): ignore all `[FACEHUGGER]` results from a single roll. | p. 76 |
| PvP timing | Players are strongly recommended to wait until the third Act before acting against the group. | p. 32 |

Note: the Breakthrough talent is "once per game session" with no Act variant [p. 77].

#### 1.5.4 Character death and replacement (Cinematic)
- PvP: GM "calls PvP" when a PvP situation escalates beyond the point of no return; involved players play the conflict to conclusion; the PC who "turned traitor", if alive, becomes an NPC under GM control; that player is given a new PC to play, if one is available [p. 32, 213].
- Death: GM normally keeps a spare character or NPC ready for that player to take over [p. 101].
- Leftover pregens are NPCs [p. 26]; in Hope's Last Day leftover PCs are played as NPCs when fewer than five players [p. 371].
- **AMBIGUOUS:** No rule on what a replacement PC inherits (Story Points stay with the player per p. 383; Agenda presumably the replacement character's own). The app should let the GM hand a player any unused pregen or surviving NPC.

#### 1.5.5 Starting conditions (Cinematic)
- Gear: the scenario determines starting gear [p. 33] (Campaign rule "two full reloads per weapon" does NOT apply).
- Buddy and Rival: set by the scenario [p. 31].
- Names: given by the scenario [p. 30].
- Vehicles/ships, if any, belong to the group [p. 33].
- STRESS LEVEL usually starts at 0 [p. 30]; Health = STRENGTH (talents may modify max) [p. 30].

### 1.6 CAMPAIGN PLAY [p. 217–219] (+ cross-references)

#### 1.6.1 Mode definition [p. 19, 217]
- Long-running, same PCs, at least 5–6 sessions, often many more. Players create their own PCs (Chapter 2). Sandbox: players decide where to go and which jobs to take [p. 19, 217–218].
- Three campaign frameworks in this book: Space Truckers, Colonial Marines, Frontier Colonists (see 2.1) [p. 19, 326].

#### 1.6.2 Session zero [p. 217]
Recommended first session: create characters together and choose the campaign framework (p. 326). Discussion questions (store as a checklist):
1. How did the group meet, and for how long have you worked as a team?
2. Who are your buddies and who are your rivals?
3. What do you do, and why do you do it?
4. Do you have a spaceship? If so, what kind? Name it.
5. What are your goals as a group?
6. Do you have any common allies or enemies?
7. Who do you work for?
8. Where are you now?

#### 1.6.3 Goals and rewards [p. 217]
- Before starting, decide why the PCs do what they do and what they work towards (examples: truckers saving to lease their own ship; marshals seeking revenge on a syndicate; marines on a tour of duty to afford a room on Anchorpoint). Goals may change. No mechanics.

#### 1.6.4 Campaign Focus [p. 217]
Give the campaign a focus (a word or sentence). Choose or roll (**AMBIGUOUS:** numbered 1–6, so D6):
| D6 | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Killing Greed |
| 2 | Corporate Intrigue |
| 3 | Tidings of War |
| 4 | New Opportunities |
| 5 | Secret Research |
| 6 | Echoes from the Past |

#### 1.6.5 A place to call home; Crawl the stars [p. 218]
- Recommended (not required): start near a home base — often the ship itself, but ideally a physical place to make connections, get job offers and relax between missions. Novgorod Station (p. 358) is provided for this.
- PCs typically start with a spaceship. Between jobs: put the star map on the table, show destinations of the current job offers; use the star system/planet generator (Chapter 12) to detail the destination.

#### 1.6.6 Creating jobs [p. 218]
- Players take jobs to earn money. Use Chapter 12 job tables for the three frameworks.
- **Always have 2–3 jobs generated** so options can be presented as soon as the current assignment ends. Present them with destinations; players choose.
- Job creation: pick or roll some complications plus **one plot twist**; build the opposition from them. NPC stats p. 354; creatures Chapter 11.

#### 1.6.7 Xenomorphs in Campaign play [p. 219]
- Cinematic: Xenomorph usually present. Campaign: do NOT encounter a lethal alien every job; use other foes (e.g. company agents, hostile troops, hired killers, rival crews, rogue androids, critters). Save the Xenomorph for special occasions. (Guidance only; the Plot Twist "Alien Outbreak" is D66 65–66, see 2.3.6.)

## 2. CAMPAIGN CHAPTER 12 (book 324–367)

General: random tables are aids; GM can pick entries, re-roll results that seem out of place, change or ignore results [p. 326, 341].

### 2.1 The campaign framework [p. 326–329]
- Choose a framework with the players (who the PCs are, their role) [p. 326].
- EMPLOYEES OR FREELANCERS? Decide early. Employees (government/corporation/military): steady (small) paycheck + access to hardware freelancers can't afford. Freelancers: less security, free to take any job, may strike it big [p. 326].
- STARTING SPACESHIP: recommended that players start with access to a ship. Employed PCs are assigned a ship and expected to take care of it. Freelancers can lease or own a ship and use earned money to repair/upgrade it (Chapter 7) [p. 327].

| Framework | Typical jobs (generator) | Suggested employers | Suggested careers | Suggested spaceship | Weekly salary | Page |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Space Truckers | Cargo runs (p. 342) | Weyland-Yutani, Lasalle Bionational, Seegson | Company Agent, Medic, Officer, Pilot, Roughneck | Model CM-88G Bison M-Class Commercial Freighter (p. 181) | $400–960 | p. 327 |
| Colonial Marines | Military missions (p. 344) | USCMC (United Americas), Space Operating Forces (UPP), Royal Marines Commandos (3WE) | Colonial Marine, Medic, Officer, Pilot, Roughneck | Conestoga-Class Frigate (p. 186) | $500–760 | p. 328 |
| Frontier Colonists ("Explorers" in tables) | Expeditions (p. 348) | Weyland-Yutani, ICSC, Colonial Marshal Bureau | Company Agent, Kid, Medic, Pilot, Roughneck, Scientist | Model CM-90S Corvus G-Class Deep Space Salvage Vessel (p. 180) | $500–640 | p. 329 |

- Colonial Marines: there is **no employer table**; orders come from Command HQ. CHAIN OF COMMAND: marines don't choose missions — GM prepares a mission and gives a briefing. For an open approach, play a mercenary company instead (free choice of missions). PCs should be the squad leaders; keep the squad small [p. 328].
- Frontier Colonists: most explorers depend on sponsors; rewards are often permits for own expeditions or knowledge [p. 329].

### 2.2 Creating star systems [p. 330–340]
Procedure for a colonized MAIN WORLD [p. 331]: (Star type) → Planet Size → Atmosphere → Temperature → Geosphere → one Planetary Terrain feature → Colony Size → Colony Mission(s) → Factions (number + nature) → Orbital Components. Optional: Colony Allegiance, Scenario Hook. Optional full system: Star System Generator (2.2.12). All listed modifiers are cumulative (**AMBIGUOUS:** cumulative stacking assumed; the book just says "add or subtract the modifiers listed below, if applicable").
- Human presence is established on the most lucrative planet, not the most Earth-like [p. 331].
- Naming: inhabited planets get names (Greek mythology / dark themes); unsurveyed bodies unnamed; surveyed (pre-colonization) bodies get a three-digit designation prefixed by an ICC code such as "LV", "MT", "RF" (e.g. RF/9, LV-562, MT-338). Suggested names list (21): Hannibal, Monos, Requiem, Nakaya, Phaeton, Nocturne, Prospero, Magdala, Hamilton, Tracatus, Aurora, Arges, Damnation, Nero, Doramin, Solitude, Euphrates, Nemesis, Moab, Steropes, Napier [p. 331].

#### 2.2.3 Atmosphere — 2D6 [p. 332–333]
| 2D6 | Type |
|---|---|
| 3 or less | Thin |
| 4–6 | Breathable |
| 7–8 | Toxic |
| 9 | Dense |
| 10 | Corrosive |
| 11 | Infiltrating |
| 12 | Special |
- Modifiers: diameter ≤4,000 km −6; 4,001–7,000 km −2 [p. 332].
- Effects [p. 333]:
  - TOXIC: Earth-like density, poisonous; not breathable without a filter mask. Without filter mask: attack with **4 Base Dice at the start of each Round**, each `[SUCCESS]` = 1 damage. If Broken: Death Roll **every Turn** until dead or brought to safety.
  - THIN: too thin to breathe (may count as vacuum). Pressure suits necessary; suit damage → explosive decompression as in vacuum (p. 107). Deep ravines may have breathable pressure (compressor masks still needed).
  - BREATHABLE: Earth density, sustains human life.
  - DENSE: not toxic but too thick to breathe (except at high altitudes). Long exposure: numbness, cramps, clots. Pressure suits must be worn; suit damage → implosion-like effect similar to vacuum exposure (p. 107).
  - CORROSIVE (toxic): without pressure suit: attack with **5 Base Dice at the start of each Round**, each `[SUCCESS]` = 1 damage; Broken → Death Roll every Turn. Pressure suits protect.
  - INFILTRATING (toxic): defeats pressure suits and airlock seals **in one Shift**; special alloys needed. Without an intact pressure suit: **6 Base Dice at the start of each Round**, each `[SUCCESS]` = 1 damage; Broken → Death Roll every Turn. Seas/rain of ammonium hydroxide, hydrofluoric/hydrochloric acid etc.
  - SPECIAL: varies across the surface or has special properties (GM); if stuck, re-roll.
- **AMBIGUOUS:** there is no "None/vacuum" atmosphere result although the Geosphere table references "No atmosphere"; treat a very thin atmosphere "that counts as vacuum" as no atmosphere (GM call).

- Game calendar: current date, days elapsed, week counter (salary, lease, maintenance), travel in progress (origin, destination, parsecs, FTL rating, arrival date), hypersleep status.
- Encounter log (star system / surface / colony), ship reactions.
- NPC roster (persistent NPCs, patrons, rivals).
- Story Points per PLAYER (cap 3). **DECIDED:** Cinematic play only (at most one per roll, skill rolls only).

### 7.2 Per session / scenario
- Session number; session date; participating players.
- Cinematic: scenario id, current Act (1–3; Hope's Last Day = single Act 3), Act start/end events, per-PC current Agenda (hidden, per Act), Agenda evaluation results per Act, Story Points awarded (and to whom; reason hidden).
- Per-Act / per-session use flags per PC: signature item relief used, The Long Haul used, Breakthrough used.
- Panic Roll results ≥13 this session (for Permanent Mental Trauma check; **DECIDED:** trauma develops on a FAILED post-session EMPATHY roll), Panic Rolls made (XP question).
- XP questions per PC (participation, agenda, buddy, rival, panic roll, danger overcome, discovery, extraordinary action, money earned).
- Game clock: Round / Turn / Shift / day counters; stealth vs combat mode.
- Scene state: zones/locations visited, locations searched (Hope's Last Day search once per location), triggered events, stress triggers already applied.
- PvP called (which PC turned NPC); PC deaths and replacements (player ↔ character mapping).
- Hope's Last Day specifics: keycards (Reynolds' destroyed; Komiskey's), Komiskey released?/admitted?, Hemming rescued?, eggs taken (dormant timer D6 Turns), live facehugger specimen carried, B2 armory opened, E2 door state (Armor 6 / 12 damage), cabinet state, sentry gun status, shuttle clamp released, hatch opened, survivors count (for sign-off, out of 158).

### 7.3 Per PC
- All character-sheet fields (4.1), plus: player, cash, age/job/personality (pregens), android flag (no Stress, no panic, no push, no signature item), max Health, current Health, Broken state, critical injuries with healing timers, conditions, consumable supply ratings, radiation, Stress Level, Buddy, Rival, Personal Agenda (Campaign: on sheet; Cinematic: hidden per Act), talents, XP unspent, gear (one item per row; tiny items separate), weapons with reloads/shots/power supply, signature item.

### 7.4 Per NPC
- Name, role/career, age (optional), appearance, trait, goal (required descriptive fields, p. 214).
- STRENGTH, AGILITY, WITS, EMPATHY (max 5 unless android), Health (current/max; default max = STRENGTH), skills (only relevant ones), talent(s) (usually none), gear (free text; consumables and ammo NOT tracked — GM decides when they run out).
- Android flag.
- Status: ACTIVE / PASSIVE (enemies), hidden/revealed, position/zone, Broken, dead, panicking (GM-declared; may add Stress to nearby PCs), captured, facehugged (+ gestation timer).
- Group membership (identical-stat NPCs share one initiative card when combatants > 10; groups act via helping rules, max +3).
- Initiative card(s).
- Flags: ex-PC (turned by PvP), patron/employer link, faction.
- No Stress Level, no Story Points, no pushing, no Panic Rolls for NPCs.
